⚖️ Legal compliance & land-access protocols

This is the heart of the request: prospecting here legally means stacking several permissions. Treat every Must item as a hard gate — going to the field without them risks penalties up to $150,000 for illegal prospecting, plus offences under the Aboriginal Heritage Act. Tick items off and assign an owner. Progress saves in your browser.

Must Each person holds a current Miner's Right
One per prospector (Steve, Charlie, Drew). A Miner's Right authorises hand-tool gold prospecting and metal detecting on Crown land, on pending/granted tenements with the holder's consent, and on private/pastoral land with the occupier's written permission. Fee ~$30, effectively lifetime. Apply online via DMPE (Dept of Mines, Petroleum & Exploration). wa.gov.au — Miner's Rights
Must Identify the registered holders of E 80/5959, 5609, 5085 & 6216
Look each tenement up in Mineral Titles Online (MTO) / TENGRAPH Web — search by number (e.g. "E80/5959") to view the Tenement Register and holder details. If unsure, phone the DMPE Mining Information Centre on (08) 9222 3626 or email mineraltitles.enquiries@dmirs.wa.gov.au. Access MTO
Must Obtain written consent from each tenement holder you'll prospect on
Prospecting on a granted Exploration Licence is not permitted without the holder's written consent — your Miner's Right alone is not enough on someone else's tenement. Contact each holder in writing, state exactly where/when you'll be and that you'll use hand tools/detectors only, and get their consent (and any conditions) in writing before you leave. Keep copies in the vehicles.
Must Native title — engage the Jaru Traditional Owners / KLC before entry
The Halls Creek / Flora Valley area is subject to the Jaru native title determination. Best practice (and often a condition of tenement access) is a heritage clearance / site-avoidance arrangement via the Jaru Prescribed Body Corporate, coordinated through the Kimberley Land Council. Contact them early — consultation takes time. klc.org.au
Must Check registered Aboriginal sites (ACHIS) & plan to avoid them
Under the Aboriginal Heritage Act 1972 it is an offence to excavate, damage or alter a registered Aboriginal site without Ministerial (s.18) consent. Search the area in the Aboriginal Cultural Heritage Inquiry System (ACHIS) before you go and mark any sites as no-go zones on your GPS. Hand-tool prospecting is low-impact, but you must steer clear of sites. DPLH — Aboriginal heritage
Must Entry/transit permit if crossing Aboriginal Lands Trust reserve land
Wunguu is an Aboriginal community at Old Flora Valley. If your route or campsite crosses a proclaimed ALT reserve, you need a free Entry/Transit Permit via the AAPA Lands Permit System (ALPS), administered by DPLH — Traditional Owners are consulted and may refuse. Apply well ahead; permits are issued business hours only. Phone 1300 651 077, email alps@dplh.wa.gov.au. DPLH — Entry permits
Must Notify / get consent from the pastoral lessee (Flora Valley / Old Flora Valley Station)
If any ground is under pastoral lease, the Mining Act lets you access it but you must not prospect on or within 400 m of any homestead, water works, dam, well or bore without the occupier's written consent, and you must leave gates as found and avoid stock. Phone ahead as a courtesy and to confirm access tracks and current station conditions.
Do Carry all paperwork (digital + printed) in both vehicles
Miner's Rights, written tenement consents, heritage/ALPS permits, pastoralist contact, marked tenement boundaries and ACHIS no-go points. If a DMPE inspector, ranger or pastoralist asks, you can show authority on the spot.
Smart Confirm rules: hand tools & detectors only — no machinery, non-commercial
A Miner's Right does not allow powered earthmoving or commercial mining. Detectors and pans are fine; you may keep gold found within your rights. Fill any holes you dig and pack out all rubbish.

Sources: WA Govt Miner's Rights & "Where you can prospect"; Mining Act 1978 (WA) s.20; DMPE Mineral Titles Online; Kimberley Land Council (Jaru determination); DPLH Aboriginal Heritage & Entry Permits (ALPS); Aboriginal Affairs Planning Authority Act 1972.

⛏ Field plan & daily routine

Rules of the ground

  • Hand tools & metal detectors only — picks, shovels, pans, sieves. No powered digging/machinery.
  • Stay inside tenements you have consent for; keep boundaries loaded on every GPS/phone.
  • Avoid all heritage sites from the ACHIS check — treat as hard exclusion zones.
  • Backfill every hole; pack out 100% of rubbish; leave gates as found.
  • Keep clear of stock, bores, dams and the homestead (400 m rule).

Suggested daily routine (dry-season heat)

  • 05:45 first light — coffee, brief the day's grid.
  • 06:30–11:30 detect/pan the cool hours (most productive).
  • 11:30–15:00 heat of day — shade, log finds, maintain gear, hydrate.
  • 15:00–18:00 second session.
  • Evening — mark productive spots on GPS, plan next grid, check comms/weather.
  • Search systematically in parallel lanes; flag and grid-out any "patch".
✓ August is the sweet spot. Dry-season days ~30–33°C, nights ~10–13°C, low rain risk, firm roads. Still: sun is intense — shade, electrolytes and ≥7 L water/person/day are non-negotiable.
