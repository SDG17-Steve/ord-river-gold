1. Wungu is confirmed and pinned

Wungu Community sits at 18.32583°S, 128.00000°E, at Old Flora Valley homestead inside Reserve 40868. Approved as a place name in 1993. It shows up in Landgate's GEONOMA database only, which is why it's missing from Wikipedia, Google and the gazetteer. Our planned camp coordinate (18.324°S, 127.998°E) is about 250 m off it, so we're camping essentially on top of the community. Plan accordingly.

2. There is gold here, but only one recorded site inside 10 km

Elvire River A (MINEDEX S0024971) is 5.8 km north-west at 18.2978°S, 127.9536°E. It's logged as an occurrence, gold only, undeveloped, with no recorded workings. That's the entire tally inside a 10 km circle.

3. The real cluster is 12–16 km west and south-west

Nineteen gold sites sit inside 20 km, and they include actual mines rather than just occurrences: Palm Springs (an open pit plus two alluvial workings, all shut), Poverty Gully 3 (shut mine), Golden Crown North and the Faugh-a-Ballagh workings on the Butchers Creek gold project. If we want ground with a proven history, that's the direction to drive.

4. Our drawn "prospecting area" isn't where the camp is

The 431 km² polygon in Prospecting_Area_Map.html has the identical bounding box to E 80/5959. It was drawn around a tenement application, not around gold. Its centroid is 27.2 km north of Wungu and the camp falls about 3 km outside its southern edge. Two different places. Worth deciding which one we're actually going to.

5. The nearest gold happens to be on legally open ground

Elvire River A sits on E 80/5085, still PENDING. Pending means the applicant holds nothing, so the land is treated as open Crown land and our Miner's Right covers it. Same story for the Elvire River deposit inside E 80/5959. Lucky, and worth not squandering.

6. The best gold in the northern area is locked

Dry Creek is a shut open-pit gold mine with six satellite workings and about 20 gold occurrences along Saunders Creek North. It's the strongest gold in the whole search. It sits on E 80/5025, which is LIVE and held by William Robert Richmond. That needs his written consent or our own Section 40E permit, and a 40E carries a 21-day notice period. There isn't time before 3 August. Treat it as closed for this trip.

Still the biggest gate: none of this touches native title. This is Jaru country and we're camping at an Aboriginal community. Tenement status decides nothing about whether we're welcome. Get the KLC / PBC response in writing before we leave.

Two traps. First: a Section 40E permit needs 21 days' notice, so anything requiring one is out of reach before 3 August. Second: pending status can flip to granted at any time, including while we're out there. The moment it does, the rules invert and we'd need written consent. Re-check every tenement on Tengraph / MTO the day before we leave, and note them down.
The practical plan: work the pending ground. Elvire River A on E 80/5085 is 5.8 km from camp, is recorded gold, and is legally open to a Miner's Right today. That's the first place to swing a detector.

⚠️ What we're not certain about

  • An earlier version of this analysis was wrong. It was built on Mindat's locality list, which is badly incomplete for this district, and concluded the area was barren Antrim Plateau basalt. MINEDEX shows otherwise. Everything on this page comes from MINEDEX and the live tenement layer instead.
  • MINEDEX records occurrences, not nuggets. There is no public database of who found what with a detector. Nobody publishes their patch. Absence of a record near camp is weak evidence of absence of gold.
  • Point-in-tenement checks were done on single coordinates. Tenement boundaries are irregular, so a site 500 m from the one we tested could sit on different ground. Verify on Tengraph before digging.
  • Historic accounts describe the Elvire as carrying "fine alluvial gold." Fine gold is effectively invisible to a detector. If that's what's there, the detectors won't see it and we'd need to be washing dirt.
  • Water is unconfirmed. It's late dry season. The sluice needs moving water and we have not verified any exists at camp. Assume it doesn't until someone checks, and bring a dry recovery option.
